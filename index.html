Why junior consultants struggle in year one

It is not a skills gap. It is a structured thinking gap, compounded by a psychological safety problem. Junior consultants do not know what they do not know, so they cannot ask the right questions. Senior consultants do not have time to teach. The gap compounds silently until it is too late.

Consulting firms are hiring more entry-level people while cutting the senior time it used to take to train them. Nobody has built the replacement for that missing training.

No straw man instinct
Senior consultants see the end output before they start. Juniors do not, and nobody teaches them to work backwards from the final slide.
Vocabulary without context
You learn what MECE means. You do not learn to apply it under pressure, in a client meeting, when the senior partner is watching.
No safe space to be wrong
There is no place to practise being wrong before the high-stakes moment. These tools are that space.

Why challenge comes first

Most AI tools only support. They validate, agree, encourage. Jazz Rasool, founder of the AI Coaching Alliance and author of Coaching 5.0, calls this pathological. Sycophantic AI leads to atrophy, not growth.

The Social Vitamins framework: three things people need to grow, in the right proportion, at the right time, from the right source. This tool is built on that architecture.

Challenge
What is wrong with your thinking. Directly. Referencing your actual words. No softening. This is where growth happens.
"You classified this as a cost problem, but three of your own diagnostic questions were about the sales team. That's a revenue hypothesis wearing a cost hypothesis's name."
See it in practice
Support
What you genuinely got right. Specific, not generic. "Good attempt" is not coaching. Real acknowledgment only.
"Your first diagnostic question isolated the variable correctly, that's exactly the instinct senior consultants build over years. Keep that."
See it in practice
Reflection
One open question. The answer is not given. You have to find it. That is how understanding is actually built.
"If the client pushed back on that hypothesis in the room right now, what's the one number you'd ask for first?"
See it in practice
